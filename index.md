<section class="section journey-section" id="journey" aria-labelledby="journey-title">
  <div class="section-heading journey-heading">
    <p class="eyebrow">05 / Learning journey</p>
    <h2 id="journey-title">A practice of<br>asking better<br>questions.</h2>
    <p class="journey-count">A learning reflection · Updated as the course progresses</p>
  </div>
  <div class="journey-copy">
    <p>My cybersecurity learning journey is still taking shape. As a Master of Cybersecurity student at Universiti Malaya, I am using WQE7001 Cyber Security to build a foundation and to become more deliberate about how I learn. I created this portfolio to keep that process visible: not just the topics I encounter, but the reasoning, evidence, and reflection that should accompany practical work.</p>
    <p>One idea I want to carry through my studies is that security is not simply a collection of tools. Tools can help us ask questions about systems, but their output needs context. A finding is meaningful only when I understand what was examined, what assumptions were made, what the evidence can and cannot show, and whether the activity was authorized. I want to develop the habit of explaining those boundaries instead of presenting a tool name or screenshot as proof by itself.</p>
    <p>For practical learning, I intend to use isolated environments and targets for which permission is explicit. Before beginning an exercise, I will identify its objective, confirm its scope, and consider how to avoid affecting systems or people outside that scope. During the work, I will keep notes that distinguish observations from interpretations. Afterwards, I will record limitations and possible next steps. This structure should make it easier to learn from mistakes and to communicate results responsibly.</p>
    <p>The projects in this portfolio are designed to support that approach. A virtual lab setup can provide a controlled place to understand system and network behavior, while a network-scanning exercise can help me study how services are identified and described. At this stage, these pages are documentation frameworks, not reports of completed experiments. I will add configurations, outputs, and conclusions only after the corresponding work has been performed and checked. If an exercise is not completed, the portfolio should say so plainly rather than imply success.</p>
    <p>I also expect my understanding to change as I encounter new concepts. When that happens, I want to revisit earlier notes and explain what I would now do differently. This is especially important in cybersecurity, where a confident conclusion based on incomplete information can be misleading. A careful account of uncertainty is more useful than an unsupported claim of expertise.</p>
    <p>Over time, I hope this record will show steady progress: clearer technical explanations, stronger evidence handling, more thoughtful risk awareness, and a better ability to connect security concepts to practical decisions. I will assess skills against specific coursework or documented practice rather than assign myself levels in advance. The goal is not to make every page look finished; it is to make the work that is shown accurate, authorized, and useful to someone reviewing how I learn.</p>
  </div>
</section>
